# Primary-source and prior-art ledger

Search/access boundary: 5 October 2026. This was a focused search using primary author versions, author/institutional repositories, web search and Exa. It is not an exhaustive bibliometric or priority audit. No claim about all citing papers is made. Full third-party articles are not redistributed in this package.

## Convex mosaics: target statement

Domokos, G., & Lángi, Z. (2022). On some average properties of convex mosaics. *Experimental Mathematics, 31*(3), 783–793. Online publication 2019.
https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2019.1691090
https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00721
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.00721
https://real.mtak.hu/114069/

Read the definitions, Conjecture 1, Problem 1, Theorem 2 and Proposition 1. The author's notation is mean node degree n-bar and mean cell degree v-bar; the supplied bundle reverses the names. The actual band is h in (d,2^(d-1)], hence (3,4] in dimension three. The finite construction satisfies convexity, face-to-face matching, normality and existence of means.

## Polytopes: construction inputs and earlier tiling work

Joswig, M., & Ziegler, G. M. (2000). Neighborly cubical polytopes. *Discrete & Computational Geometry, 24*, 325–344.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s004540010039
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9812033
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9812033

Read the cubical existence theorem and deformed-cube construction. For a neighborly cubical four-polytope with the n-cube graph, f0=2^n, f3=(n-2)2^(n-2), f03=8f3. The finite n=6 specialization in this package is checked directly at epsilon=1/2, rather than trusting an unquantified small-parameter theorem.

Ziegler, G. M. (2002). Face numbers of 4-polytopes and 3-spheres. In *Proceedings of the International Congress of Mathematicians, Beijing 2002* (Vol. III, pp. 625–634). Higher Education Press. Revised author version 2003.
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208073
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208073

IMPORTANT PRIOR ART: Section 7 explicitly gives the Schlegel-diagram insertion into tetrahedral space tilings, discusses high mean vertex/tile degrees, and stresses the liftability issue for a converse. Section 4 discusses normalized complexity and the inequality f03 - 3(f0+f3) >= -10. The package credits the insertion method and qualitative high-degree constructions. Its equivalence theorem is explicitly restricted to periodic weighted-Delaunay/Laguerre mosaics so that the converse lifting exists.

Ziegler, G. M. (2004). Projected products of polygons. *Electronic Research Announcements of the American Mathematical Society, 10*, 122–134.
https://doi.org/10.1090/S1079-6762-04-00137-4
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0407042
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0407042

The arXiv metadata title is “Projected Products of Polytopes”; the manuscript/PDF title is “Projected Products of Polygons.” Read Theorem 1.1 and the facet-type count proof of Proposition 5.1. The needed counts are a=m^r, b=(r-2)m^r/4+r*m^(r-1), j=4(r-1)m^r. The cube-facet count is (r-2)m^r/4 and the prism-facet count is r*m^(r-1). These prove the stated asymptotic harmonic degree 16 after the bounded two-stack port cost. The package uses these counts, not the extraneous f2 entry in the preprint's displayed flag vector; that displayed entry is inconsistent with Euler's equation as printed. Likewise, raw j/(a+b) and normalized complexity must not be conflated.

## Abrasion: target correction and analytic input

Domokos, G. (2015). Monotonicity of spatial critical points evolving under curvature-driven flows. *Journal of Nonlinear Science, 25*, 247–275. Online publication 2014.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00332-014-9228-3
https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4779
https://arxiv.org/html/1308.4779v4

Read the introduction, Assumptions 1 and 2, equations (49)–(53), Conjecture 1, and the separate Assumptions 2-A and 3. The published conjecture concerns N-bar, an expected count. Deterministic critical-point creation on surfaces is already acknowledged, and a heat-equation example is given. The finite probability law here directly contradicts the inference from zero means/covariance to the sign conclusion in equation (51), including at positive equal curvature coefficients. It is not a claim against all stronger stochastic assumptions.

Huisken, G. (1984). Flow by mean curvature of convex surfaces into spheres. *Journal of Differential Geometry, 20*(1), 237–266.
https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214438998
https://maths.anu.edu.au/files/CMAProcVol8-Huisken_1.pdf

The author lecture gives the smooth short-time existence and strictly convex mean-curvature-flow setting. The companion also explicitly displays the positive-definite principal coefficient of each support-function PDE, so that the local uniformly parabolic existence/parameter-dependence input is identifiable. No long-time convergence theorem or numerical PDE integration is needed for the birth argument.

## Search conclusion

A targeted resolution search did not identify an earlier article explicitly presenting this harmonic-degree counterexample or these exact certificates. That negative search result is not a proof of priority. The substantial older Schlegel/tiling and critical-point-creation literature is credited. Before public novelty claims, an independent specialist should check whether the later harmonic-degree implication or the exact abrasion correction has already been recorded elsewhere.
